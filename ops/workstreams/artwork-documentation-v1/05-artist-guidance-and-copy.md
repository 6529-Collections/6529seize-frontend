# Artist guidance and product copy

Parent: [index](README.md). This is ready-to-implement English source copy, version `artwork-documentation-copy-v1`. Implement with translation keys and adapt layout without changing the claims. This package does not send an invitation or publish marketing.

## Publication-only intake and worked examples

Version 2 of each documentation profile prepares one artwork record intended
for publication with the work. Its artwork answers and selected files have no
per-field privacy choice. Legacy version 1 retains its existing choices; no
restricted material is silently republished or copied into a new record.
Draft storage is temporary preparation, not publication or minting.

Every visible section offers a coherent, clearly fictional worked example:
*The Space Between*, by Ari Example, is a composite of two photographs of a
handmade cardboard gate. Its complete answers cover all eight modules. Empty
sections open the example; completed sections keep it available. Examples
follow supported profile fields and the pinned interview instrument. They
never become artist data merely by opening or reviewing them.

Selected empty narrative fields offer a `[[prompt]]` writing structure in a
local, unsaved editor. The artist replaces the prompts and explicitly applies
their own schema-valid answer through ordinary draft saving. Facts, identities,
rights, permissions, dates and asset references have no one-click insertion.
Applying a structure cannot overwrite an answer already present in the form.

**Questions for the team** is a separate drafting conversation at the end of
review. It uses context-wide comments outside artwork modules, snapshots and
publication previews. Resolve uncertainties before finalizing and move only
the relevant public answer into the artwork form. It is not an evidence
deposit or a private appendix to the eventual decentralized record.

The earlier private-evidence and visibility copy below applies only to legacy
version 1. Publication-only helpers supersede those invitations for version 2.

## 1. The story we are telling

Lead with the artist's work and voice. A title and an image can travel widely while the circumstances, choices and history behind them disappear. This feature helps artists keep those things together. Photography particularly benefits from distinguishing the finished image from source files, remembering how an image was constructed and recording the conditions under which it should be seen.

Describe practical benefits: accurate credits, the artist's own account, clearer future exhibition/research, and better information for preserving and displaying a work. Avoid implying that filling a form proves authenticity or guarantees market value. “Museum-quality documentation” can describe the ambition in editorial discussion; the product must not award “museum grade,” “verified authentic” or “certified” status from completion.

### Workspace introduction

**Your work has a history. Keep it with the work.**

“Tell us how you made it, what it means to you and what future viewers should know. Your words, source files and display preferences help people understand the work on your terms.”

“Start with what you have. Your progress is saved with 6529, and you can return to add more. We will ask you to review and confirm a specific version when you are ready.”

Storage note, visible beside the introduction:

“Your documentation is stored privately with 6529 for now. You choose which material is intended for a future public record. Nothing is published on-chain or to IPFS/Arweave by completing this form.”

Primary action: **Start documenting**. Returning action: **Continue documentation**. Secondary action: **See what we will ask**. That link reveals the six groups, estimated effort described qualitatively and required/recommended distinctions; it does not open a policy PDF.

### Inline submission introduction

**Add the story behind your work**

“A few words now can help preserve the choices and context behind the image. Add what you like here, or return after submitting.”

Actions: **Add documentation** / **Continue later**.

Supporting sentence: “These optional documentation fields do not change whether your submission can be sent.” Show this only where the profile is optional under v1; never reuse it for a future mandatory flow without changing the configuration and copy.

### Keys and Gates introduction

**Help establish the record for Keys and Gates.**

“Keys and Gates is the Network Museum's first commissioned project and is planned as the first Stream release. Alongside the photograph, we want to preserve your account of making it: what you saw, the choices you made and what matters when the work is shown.”

“We have brought across available information from your submission so you can check it, rather than write it all again. You can work through this in stages. An honest ‘I don't know’ is more useful than a reconstructed answer.”

“This step prepares the artwork's documentation. Mint arrangements will be handled separately.”

The planned Stream-release sentence reflects the current product decision, not a claim of an existing deployment or completed accession. Recheck tense before any external publication.

## 3. Visibility copy

**Intended for public record** — “You would like this included in a future public record. It remains private here for now. Publication will be a separate step.”

**Restricted** — “Available only to you and participants specifically authorized for this information.” Use a “Who can access this?” disclosure listing current people/roles the caller may see, with a route to revoke editor access where applicable.

**Withheld** — “We will record that you chose not to supply this information. Do not enter the private value in the explanation.”

**Private preview** — “This shows material marked for a future public record. Nothing is published. Restricted information is omitted.”

For archival and consent uploads, state actual access independently of intended publication. A final artwork can be intended for public presentation while its uploaded original is still restricted. Do not display a padlock beside a publicly accessible URL.

## 4. Rights copy for the pilot

“Keys and Gates specifies CC0 1.0 for the artwork. CC0 is intended to permit broad reuse, including commercial reuse, without asking permission. It does not remove rights held by other people, such as privacy or publicity rights. Read the [CC0 explanation](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/) and the program's terms before confirming your intended release.”

“Record whether your declaration is proposed, conditional or already effective. Saving or confirming this documentation does not itself issue a new CC0 instrument. Any existing grant remains governed by the original declaration.”

“Your biography, private evidence, source files and interview are separate materials. We will record their intended terms and access separately.”

If people are depicted: “The program requires written consent for depicted people and excludes identifiable minors. Tell us what evidence you have so the team can review it privately.” Do not offer an “I accept the risk” override or present an uploaded parental release as automatically satisfying the exclusion. Do not generate legal release forms in this delivery.

## 6. Optional interview instrument

Instrument ID: `artwork-documentation-artist-interview-v1`, version 1. Each answer is optional and supports original language plus attributed translations. Suggest conversational answers; do not impose an essay target.

1. What first drew you to make this work?
2. What is happening in the image, and what lies outside the frame that a future viewer should know?
3. Which choices in capture, construction or editing were most important to the final work?
4. How does this work relate to your practice and to other works in the same series?
5. For Keys and Gates, what does the idea of a key or a gate mean in this image? Other programs use their own versioned thematic prompt.
6. What is most likely to be misunderstood about this work?
7. What must remain intact when the work is displayed, reproduced or preserved?
8. Is there anything you would want someone encountering the work many years from now to know?

Before a recording/transcript is added, identify participants, date and permissions. The UI states whether it is private review material or intended for a future public record. Artist corrections remain attributable. A declined interview records that choice; it does not fabricate a transcript or legal waiver.

## 7. Artist FAQ and launch language

**Do I have to finish everything now?** “No. Start with the artwork and the information you already have. The workspace separates required review questions from recommended material, and saves your progress as you work.”

**Can I do this after submitting?** “Yes. Open your submission or My artwork documentation and continue the same record.”

**Can I change it later?** “Yes. Changes create a new working version. Earlier confirmed versions remain identifiable. Any later change to a published or minted record would be handled separately.”

**Will everyone see my files?** “No. This workspace is private to authorized participants. Original files and consent evidence have additional access restrictions. You can see the intended publication choice and access information alongside the material.”

**What if I do not know an answer?** “Use the available unknown or unavailable option and explain what you can. The team may follow up where the program needs a clearer answer.”

**Does completing this mean my work is minted or accessioned?** “No. Documentation, review, minting and Museum accession are separate steps.”

**Why does this matter if the image is already online?** “The image is one part of the work's record. Your account, credits, history, source material and display guidance help future people understand what they are looking at and what you intended.”

Proposed short feature description for later approved launch use:

> Artwork documentation brings the artist's voice, process and preservation guidance together with the work. Begin when you submit, add more as the story develops, and help future viewers understand the choices behind the image.

Avoid promised investment returns, guaranteed permanence, automatic authenticity verification and unsupported “first ever” claims. Market the actual care taken with the record and the value of the artist's contribution.
