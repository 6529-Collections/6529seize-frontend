## Summary

This is the single frontend feature PR for guided four-wallet consolidation, the TDH collector guide and simulator, and Safe Collecting with its TAP guide and wallet tools. It includes the former parent PRs and uses environment-specific activation timing and fixed wallet limits supplied by backend settings.

## What Changed

The entire public route family is under `/safe-collecting`: TAP Guide, FAQ and its articles, Consolidation Use Cases, Wallet Checker, Guided Setup, registration forms, primary-address actions and collection management. Former `/delegation` URLs permanently redirect with query parameters and section anchors preserved. All internal links, metadata, sitemap and help knowledge use canonical destinations. Search aliases let both `delegation` and `delegations` find the five main pages without changing their visible names.

The redundant NFT Delegation About page and navigation entry are removed; its former URL redirects to the hub. About retains `Safe Collecting`, with Primary Address last. GDRC is the final Collections & Minting item. FAQ chrome is labelled `FAQ`; technical action and article titles retain delegation terminology. The unpublished documentation bundle is rebuilt with updated links and integrity hashes.

Guided Setup plans reciprocal links for two to four wallets, orders existing members first, batches a wallet's own directions and supports managers signing one link per transaction. Wallet validation, prefill, profile entry points, signing eligibility and link freshness consume the backend limits and cutoff. Missing required policy pauses signing with a retry, and policy changes discard the previous planning baseline.

The collector guide at `/network/tdh/consolidation` explains roles, TDH benefits, full-mesh consent and signing order. Its design page and simulator at `/network/tdh/consolidation/design` illustrate post-cutoff transactions using the selected backend's policy. The TAP guide uses the same displayed maximum and supplies neutral guidance while limits are unavailable. No local limit constant or new secret is introduced. The help corpus and desktop troubleshooting docs explain Core’s separate wait for backend policy, preserved TDH results and full regrouping on policy changes, with updated source provenance.

TDH documentation and help knowledge also explain ordinal ranks, the overall, collection and per-Meme comparison criteria, and the lowercase wallet-address/consolidation-key tie-break shared with native Core. Equal scores do not receive a shared rank; tie-breaks change positions without changing scores or boosts. Collection rank `-1`, eligible zero-score wallets and Gradient/NextGen token ordering are documented.

Profiles display all four wallets and a right-aligned, settings-driven wallet count. The owner can add a wallet below capacity and sees a limit note at capacity. Wallet Checker and consolidated stats retain complete four-wallet coverage. The Addresses/ID Statements layout from [FE #4290](https://github.com/6529-Collections/6529seize-frontend/pull/4290) is included from main: the personalized Addresses heading retains the right-aligned count, wallet actions share equal-width columns, and the ID Statements information/Add controls stay below the addresses. Shared staging and production profile coverage preserves both layout contracts and the Safe Collecting destinations.

Registration and primary-address pages provide `Back to Safe Collecting` during wallet restoration and while disconnected, including with a remembered signed-in profile. Shared form close buttons use the site's standard portal tooltip with hover and keyboard-focus support. A subtle note beneath the builder's wallet counter shows the selected environment's four-wallet signing opening time only before the activation cutoff and disappears at the cutoff in an open tab. The note uses a compact UTC date and the available width, keeping UTC attached to the time. Advance planning and signing eligibility are unchanged.

Guided Setup reuses Wallet Checker’s ENS input. Names are forward-resolved on mainnet before planning, with canonical addresses used for grouping and signing. Editing a name clears its prior resolved address; unresolved names and duplicate aliases block planning.

## Dependencies / Rollout

The counter notice's visibility is isolated from signing eligibility, and redirect generation retains Next.js's Promise-returning contract.

No additional cutoff constant or frontend environment variable is introduced by this PR.
